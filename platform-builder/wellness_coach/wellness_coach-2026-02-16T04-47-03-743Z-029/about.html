Quick myths I correct first

I use a short diagnostic and evidence-based coaching to replace long-standing myths with practical truths.

Myth: Willpower fixes everything

Truth: Systems and micro-habits reduce reliance on raw willpower. We design triggers and feedback that make desired actions obvious.

Myth: Quick fixes are sustainable

Truth: Repetition, measurement, and progressive overload produce long-term retention — we focus on incremental dosing.

Myth: One-size coaching fits all

Truth: You get a modular framework tuned to your schedule, biology-friendly routines, and measurable milestones.

Three pillars of the method

Assessment

Micro-routines

Adaptive feedback

Weekly data reviews and protocol adjustments — you learn what scales and what stalls.

Questions I hear most

How quickly will I see changes?

Many clients notice habit stability within 3–6 weeks; measurable performance shifts are often visible by 8–12 weeks depending on the target.

Is this medical advice?

No — coaching is behavioral and educational. I do not diagnose or treat medical conditions; I coordinate with healthcare providers when relevant.

What tools do you use?

Simple tracking (journals, habit apps, or spreadsheet metrics), brief weekly check-ins, and a modular playbook we iterate together.

Ready to test a small intervention?

Start with a free 7-day Habit Starter — a pragmatic worksheet and two micro-routines you can implement this week. No fluff, just experiments.
