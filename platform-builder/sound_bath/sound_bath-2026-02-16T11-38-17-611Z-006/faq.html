Common questions

Everything you want to know before you arrive

Clear, warm answers for first-timers and returning guests.
What happens in a typical session?
We begin with a short intake and breathwork to align the nervous system. Next, layers of sound — a slow build using the chosen instruments — move through 45–60 minutes of active listening. We close with slow re-entry and integration prompts. For "intensive" bookings expect a longer, intentional structure with post-session coaching and journaling prompts.
What should I bring?
Bring a yoga mat, a thick blanket, a small pillow for under your knees or head, and a light eye covering. Wear comfortable layers, and bring water. We recommend leaving phones on silent and in your bag.
Contraindications — is it safe for everyone?
Most people enjoy sound baths, but please contact us before booking if you are pregnant, have an inner-ear condition, epilepsy, recent surgery, or medical implants. Loud gongs or specific low frequencies may be avoided on request. Our facilitators will adapt the session to prioritize safety.
Can I move, cry, or make noise?
Yes. We hold space for all responses. Movement and vocal expression can be part of the healing process; please remain mindful of the group container if in a shared circle.
What instruments do you use?
Our palette rotates; common instruments include a gong, Tibetan singing bowls, the monochord, and soft chimes. We choose combinations to support intention and the energetic arc of each session.

How to prepare for an intensive
For our intensive model we request you arrive rested, hydrate well, and bring a journal. The intensive often includes pre-session questions, 90–120 minutes of focused sound work, and a brief follow-up integration check-in within 7 days.
