Myth vs. Truth

Myth: Sound baths are like loud concerts.

Truth: Our gatherings are curated, low-volume soundscapes designed for inward listening and safety.

Myth: You must meditate perfectly.

Truth: No experience required. The sound carries you — resting, daydreaming, or sleeping is all welcomed.

Myth: Results happen instantly.

Truth: Many feel immediate calm; deeper integration often arrives over days. Consistency helps.

What to expect — the flow

Arrival & settling

We greet you, guide gentle grounding, and offer a short centering breath practice so bodies arrive as they are.

Sound immersion

An hour of layered tones — bowls, gong washes, chimes — moving through frequency that soothes the nervous system.

Return & integration

We close slowly: a soft bell, time to journal or sip herbal tea, and a brief check-in for any questions.

Practical — what to bring & health note

What to bring
  • Yoga mat, blanket, and cosy pillows
  • Layered clothing, eye mask, water bottle
  • Optional: small bolsters or neck support
Contraindications

If you are pregnant, have a pacemaker, recent surgery, or severe uncontrolled epilepsy, please consult your physician and contact us before booking. Sound can intensify sensations; we provide alternatives on request.

Case notes & responses

Inner recalibration

Participants often report slowed breathing, vivid imagery, and a softening of chronic tension after a single session.

Sleep improvement

Several attendees note deeper sleep over the following nights — not guaranteed, but a frequent pattern.

Group coherence

Shared silence and resonance create a gentle social reset: less reactivity, more calm connection.

Frequently asked

Do I need experience?

No. We design the space for beginners and seasoned listeners alike.

Yes — resting is part of the practice. You can leave quietly when ready.

Are recordings available?

Live presence is central. We occasionally offer recorded short meditations for home practice.
