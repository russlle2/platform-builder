Practical FAQ

How early should I arrive?
Plan to arrive 10–15 minutes early for check-in and to settle into your mat so the session can start punctually.
What should I bring?
Bring a thick yoga mat or blanket, a small cushion/pillow for head support, a light blanket for warmth, and a water bottle. Avoid heavy scents.
What instruments will be used?
We create textured soundscapes; typical instruments in our gatherings include crystal singing bowls, a bowed monochord, wind chimes, and a handpan. Each event emphasizes different timbres.
Is the experience suitable for beginners?
Yes. No prior experience is required. Sessions are guided and accessible; you may rest or gently observe sensations as they arise.
Are there contraindications?
People with implanted cardiac devices (pacemakers), those who have had a recent concussion, or are in acute psychiatric crisis should consult a clinician before attending. Pregnant attendees in the first trimester should seek medical guidance. If in doubt, email us at {{EMAIL}}.
Can I fall asleep?
Absolutely. Falling asleep is common and a fine outcome — we hold space and gently wake the room at the session's end.
How do you accommodate accessibility needs?
If mobility or sensory needs require adjustments (chair seating, proximity to exits, lowered volume), please let us know during booking so we can prepare.

Flow overview

Each session is intentionally paced: arrival & grounding, a brief orientation, a sustained soundscape (40–55 minutes), then a slow transition back to ambient sound and a short period for tea or quiet reflection.

Noise sensitivity

Tonal layers can include low frequency. If you are sensitive to loud or sustained tones, contact us for quieter placement in the room.

Short health note

This practice is complementary; it is not medical treatment. If you are on seizure medication, have epilepsy, or a diagnosed condition that sound may affect, consult a clinician first.
