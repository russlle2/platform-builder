Frequently asked questions

Concise answers for your first visit — sensory information, safety and how membership works.

What happens in a typical session?

You arrive, settle into a cushioned nest, then we guide a slow detuning of attention. Sounds—often gong strikes, tuning forks and sustained singing bowls—are layered to create a map for breathing and sensation. After the soundscape we allow a gentle, silent return before closing.

How long is the session and what is the flow?

Doors open 20 minutes early. The group gathers for a 10–15 minute grounding introduction, then 45–60 minutes of soundwork, followed by 5–10 minutes of integration and optional sharing.

What should I bring?

Comfortable clothing, a mat or cushion, a light blanket and an eye mask if you like. We recommend leaving jewelry with high resonance in your bag if it distracts you.

Are there any safety considerations?

Please tell us if you have epilepsy, implanted medical devices, severe psychiatric history, or pregnancy concerns. Loud gong hits are modulated; we can offer a quieter placement if needed. Always consult your health provider about participation if uncertain.

How is membership different?

Members reserve spots before drop-in availability, receive discounted rates, and have invitation-only mini-sessions with binaural recordings and guided micro-practices.

Can I bring a friend or children?

Sessions are curated for adults seeking quiet and restorative practice. Children under 16 require prior arrangement and supervision. Guests of members are welcome but should register in advance.

Quick notes

Instruments we often bring: gong, tuning forks and Himalayan singing bowls. Each session mixes timbres to avoid the same palette twice.
