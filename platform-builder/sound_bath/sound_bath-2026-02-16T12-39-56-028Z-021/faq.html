Questions that come often

Brief answers for the curious. If you need a longer conversation, email {{EMAIL}} or call {{PHONE}}.

What happens during a sound bath?
We begin with arrivals and gentle breath guidance so bodies settle. The facilitator then layers tones — a slow, intentional arc: grounding drones, melodic bowls, intermittent chimes — guiding attention and intermissions for rest. The whole session ends with a soft reconnection and space to integrate.
How should I prepare?
Bring a mat, blanket, and an eye cover if you like. Wear warm, comfortable layers. We recommend silence on the way here when possible to arrive in a quieter state.
What to bring — quick list
Yoga mat or blanket; pillow/bolster; socks; water bottle; optional eye mask; phone silenced.
Contraindications & safety
If you are pregnant, have epilepsy, implanted medical devices, recent head/ear surgery, severe psychiatric conditions, or are under acute medical care, consult your provider before attending. Live sound can be intense — tell us ahead and we can adapt. For direct concerns contact {{EMAIL}}.
Can I fall asleep?
Yes — and that can be part of the work. We encourage gentle awareness if you plan to integrate imagery or somatic noticing. Waking is soft; we leave time for gradual return.
Do you play loud instruments?
We carefully shape dynamics. The gong appears sometimes for a broader sonic field; when present, it's introduced with warning and lower volume. Hearing protection is available on request.
How long are sessions?
Group sessions are commonly 60–90 minutes; private sessions vary. See pricing for exact timings.
