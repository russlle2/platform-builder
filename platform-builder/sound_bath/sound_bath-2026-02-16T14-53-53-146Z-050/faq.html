Common practical questions

Below are short, clear answers to the most frequent questions. If something is missing, contact us at {{EMAIL}}.

What should I bring?

A mat or blanket, a pillow for your head and knees, comfortable layered clothing, a water bottle and an eye cover if you prefer complete darkness.

Are sound baths safe?

For most people, yes. Please read the contraindications: if you have a pacemaker, recent head/ear surgery, seizure condition, or are in the first trimester of pregnancy, consult your healthcare provider and notify the facilitator prior to the session.

What instruments are used?

We select instruments to create layered textures — expect tuning forks, a deep monochord drone, and an ocean drum for subtle surface rhythms.

How does a session flow?

An hour typically: arrival and orientation, a guided body and breath scan, a sustained sound journey (30–45 minutes), slow return and brief integration. Sessions prioritize silence after sound to deepen resonance.

Accessibility & comfort

We welcome mobility aids and can adapt seating. If you need specific support, email {{EMAIL}} or call {{PHONE}} so we can prepare the venue and layout.

Booking & policies

Drop-ins available; reserves recommended. Cancellations within 48 hours may be subject to a fee. Private and corporate bookings require a deposit.

Short contraindication primer

If you use implanted medical devices or have recently had surgery, please consult your provider. Loud sustained gongs can be modulated; we will accommodate requests for reduced volume or instrument choices. If you experience dizziness, leave the mat slowly and sit for a few minutes.

Micro practices to extend the work

  • Daily 90-second humming to revive vagal tone.
  • Two-minute conscious listening: close your eyes and track three ambient layers.
  • Evening low-light ritual: 5 minutes of slow exhales while holding a warm cup of tea.
