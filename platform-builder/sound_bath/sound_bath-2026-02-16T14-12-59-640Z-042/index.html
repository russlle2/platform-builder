Myth vs. truth — a quiet guide

Myth

Sound baths are background music for relaxation only — soft and unstructured.

Truth

Every session is intentional: layered instruments, measured pacing, and cues that support safe embodiment. We design a flow that takes you from arrival to rest to gentle return.

Three pillars of our gatherings

  • Sonic architecture

    We arrange tone and space — from deep gong valleys to crystalline overtone fields — so sound becomes a structure you can inhabit.

  • Somatic care

    Clear guidance, pacing for nervous systems, and accessible options make the work restorative and safe.

  • Intentional ritual

    Simple prompts and a steady sequence create a reliable frame for letting go and integration.

What a session feels like

  1. Arrive & settle: soft lighting, grounding breath, intention-setting.
  2. Entry: warm, low tones invite the body to release and soften.
  3. Immersion: layered instruments — waves of sound that create a felt landscape.
  4. Return: gentle reconnection with breath and light movement; a short integration period.

What to bring

  • Yoga mat or low-profile mattress
  • Blanket and small pillow for neck support
  • Water bottle and an open heart
  • Eye mask or cosy scarf (optional)

Contraindications: If you are pregnant, have a history of epilepsy, severe cardiac conditions, or are sensitive to intense sound, please consult your healthcare provider and contact us before attending. Some instruments may produce strong vibrations; facilitators adapt sessions as needed.

Questions folks often ask

Is this session therapeutic or medical?

These gatherings are not a substitute for medical or mental health care. They are somatic and sensory experiences intended for relaxation and integration.

How long is a typical event?

Our public events run ~75 minutes: arrival (10–15 min), sound immersion (50–60 min), gentle return (10–15 min).

Can I move during the session?

Yes. Many participants rest, some shift posture, and a few move gently. You are invited to follow your comfort and the facilitator's cues.
