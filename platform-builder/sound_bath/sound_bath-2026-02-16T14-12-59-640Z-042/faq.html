Common questions

What should I bring?

Comfort is paramount. Bring a mat or a thick blanket, a small pillow or bolsters, layered clothing, and water. An eye pillow is helpful; we sometimes provide simple cloth eye covers.

Will I fall asleep?

Many people fall into deep rest; others remain awake and attentive. There is no right way to be. The session is effective even if you sleep.

Are sound baths therapeutic?

They are supportive for relaxation and sleep improvement, and they can reduce stress markers. They are not a replacement for medical or mental health treatment but can complement ongoing care.

What are the phases of a session?

We begin with arrival and gentle alignment, move into breath-led intention, unfold a layered sound journey, then guide a gradual reorientation. Expect time at the end to share light tea and reflections.

How loud will it be?

We modulate volume intentionally. Instruments like the gong are used with restraint. If you are sensitive, we provide alternatives and seating further from sources.

Contraindications — who should check with a provider?

Consult with a clinician if you have a history of epilepsy or seizures, recent head trauma, unmanaged cardiovascular conditions, or if you are pregnant and have concerns. Also advise us if you have implants, pacemakers, or cochlear devices.

Quick notes

Instruments vary by set. We favor soft resonance: a recent mix included a gong, antique bell chimes, and tuning forks for microtonal layering. Other times we use large crystal bowls, frame drum, and harmonium-like textures.

Booking & cancellations

A 24-hour cancellation is requested for public events. Private sessions have separate policies—see the booking page.

Accessibility

We hold accessible mats and seating. Tell us your needs when you reserve and we'll adapt the layout.

When to speak with the facilitator before the session

  • You're pregnant or postpartum and uncertain about vibration exposure.
  • You have a history of seizures or fainting.
  • You use an implanted medical device or cochlear implant.
  • You have severe claustrophobia or sensory processing sensitivities.

What to bring — quick checklist

Mat/blanket, pillow, eye cover, water, comfy layers, any meds you might need. We provide extra blankets on request.
