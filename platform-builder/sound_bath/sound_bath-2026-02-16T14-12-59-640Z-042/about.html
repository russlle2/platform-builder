A few quietly important things

Myth vs. truth — sound as medicine

Myth: Sound baths are mystical entertainment.
Truth: They're structured sessions with clear phases—arrival, breath work, guided rest, and gentle reorientation—backed by somatic principles and safety checks.
Myth: Everyone sleeps through them.
Truth: While deep rest is common, many people report altered perception, vivid imagery, eased pain, and improved sleep after a few sessions.

How a typical session flows

Our events follow a gentle arc: arrival and settling (10–15 min), breath-led calibration and intention (5–10 min), layered soundscape (35–45 min), and soft return with tea and time to integrate (10–15 min).

Instruments today: crystal bowls, wind chimes, soft frame drum—selected to support resonance without overwhelm.

Guiding pillars

Safety first

We screen for sensitivity to strong vibrations and provide alternatives for anyone with hearing devices, certain implants, pregnancy, or seizure disorders.

Intentionality

Every set has a beginning, middle and end. We use intention setting and breath-work to help participants anchor their experience.

Short FAQs

What to bring

A yoga mat or blanket, a pillow, water, and an eye covering. Wear comfortable layers—temperature often drops during rest.

Contraindications

Not recommended for people with uncontrolled epilepsy, recent head injury, or those advised by a medical provider to avoid strong vibrations. If you have implants or are pregnant, tell the facilitator before the session.
