Myths & truths about sound baths

Myth

Sound baths are only for deeply spiritual people.

Truth

They are accessible sensory practices — helpful for stress, sleep, and focus for many backgrounds.

Myth

You must do something specific to "receive" a sound bath.

Truth

No technique required — rest, breathe, and allow sound to be the attention anchor.

Myth

Effects are immediate and permanent.

Truth

Many people notice immediate calm; repeated practice supports integration over time.

Our pillars — how we design sessions

Safety & consent

Clear guidance, optional movement, and pre-session intake for any sensitivities.

Sensory architecture

A curated progression of timbre and space using instruments like crystal bowls, tuned chimes, and the monochord to shape immersion.

Integration

Gentle closing cues, optional journaling prompts, and resources to carry the calm into daily life.

Frequently asked

Is a sound bath safe during pregnancy?
Many pregnant people attend with facilitator approval. We recommend checking with your care provider if you have concerns. We offer lower-intensity options and seating if needed.
Do I need experience?
No. Sessions are guided for first timers and repeat attendees alike. Rest, notice, and breathe.
Are sound baths therapeutic / medical?
Our work is somatic and supportive but not a substitute for medical or psychiatric care. If you have a history of seizures, epilepsy, or severe psychiatric conditions, consult your clinician before attending and inform the facilitator.
Contraindications & safety notes
High-volume exposure is avoided. If you have a pacemaker, tinnitus, pregnancy complications, recent surgery, or are on heavy medication, complete the intake form and check with your provider. We offer quiet seats and distance from instruments.
What instruments are used?
We rotate instruments: crystal bowls, gong, chimes, tuning forks, and the monochord. Instrument choice adapts to the session intention.
How to prepare
Avoid heavy meals right before. Hydrate, wear comfortable layers, and arrive 10–15 minutes early to settle. Bring what helps you rest for 60–90 minutes.
