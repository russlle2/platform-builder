Why a sound bath helps

Deep relaxation

Resonant harmonic tones invite the nervous system to down-regulate — lowering stress hormones and promoting restorative sleep cycles.

Embodied presence

Guided breathwork and sustained vibrations help anchor attention in the body, making the experience somatic, not merely intellectual.

Vibrational clarity

Precise instruments (crystal bowls, chimes, and tuning forks) are tuned to support clarity, balance, and a sense of renewed focus.

What to expect — session flow

  1. Arrival & settling (10m) — soft tea, lay down on your mat, eye-mask offered.
  2. Grounding (10m) — gentle breath cues, intention setting led by the facilitator.
  3. Main immersion (40–50m) — waves of sound from crystal bowls, chimes, and a singular gong bloom; layered textures to support release.
  4. Transition back (10m) — grounding tones, silence, and a soft vocal prompt to return gently.
  5. Closing & integration (5–10m) — brief reflection, sharing optional, next steps for home practice.

What to bring

  • Comfortable layers, a yoga mat, bolster or cushion, eye-mask or scarf.
  • A light blanket for warmth — we keep the room cool for comfort.
  • Optional journal for insights afterward.

Contraindications

Sound baths are generally safe, but not recommended during the first trimester of pregnancy, after recent head injury, or directly after surgery without medical clearance. If you have epilepsy, severe auditory sensitivities, or implanted medical devices, consult your clinician before attending. Contact us at {{EMAIL}} with any questions.
