Questions people ask before they arrive

Short answers for preparing well. If you need personal guidance, reach out at {{EMAIL}}.

How long is a typical sound bath?
Events usually run 70–90 minutes. We open with a short centering (10–15 minutes), move through the main soundscape (40–60 minutes) and close slowly with integration and tea. Arrival 15–20 minutes early is recommended.
What should I bring?
Bring a yoga mat or thick blanket to lie on, a pillow for neck support, a light blanket to keep warm, and an eye-sock or sleep mask if you prefer darkness. Comfortable layers are best. Please leave large valuables at home.
Is a sound bath safe? Any contraindications?
Most people find sound baths gentle and restorative. However: if you are pregnant, have epilepsy, a pacemaker/implanted electronic device, recent surgeries, or are acutely unwell, consult your healthcare provider before attending and let us know in advance. We keep volume levels moderate and accommodate individual needs — tell the facilitator when you arrive.
What happens during a session? Describe the flow.
The evening follows a soft arc: welcome & settling, an opening practice to tune attention, the main sound journey where instruments create layered resonance, and a slow return with space for quiet reflection. You are invited to rest — most people lie down; some sit — and simply receive the sound.
Which instruments are used?
We choose instruments to shape texture and movement. Typical elements include: gong for spacious washes, tuning forks for precise harmonics, handpan for rounded percussion, and soft metallic chimes for shimmering high overtones.
Can I record or bring a camera?
We ask that participants refrain from recording to preserve the intimate container. Photographs of the room taken before the session are fine; please silence devices.
