Common questions

What is a sound bath and how does it help?
A sound bath is a guided session where resonant instruments create a sustained sonic environment. The focused tones help shift attention away from worry and toward interoception — often producing lowered heart rate and increased parasympathetic activity. Sessions are supportive, non-diagnostic, and intended for wellbeing.
How long should I expect the session to be?
Typical group events last 60–80 minutes in total (settling, sound, and re-orientation). Private sessions may be scheduled for 45–120 minutes depending on goals.
What should I bring?
Bring a comfortable mat or blanket, pillow, and a light cover. Avoid heavy scents. Earplugs are welcome if you prefer reduced intensity. Hydrate before and after.
Contraindications and safety
Do not attend if you have uncontrolled seizures, recent traumatic brain injury, or severe sound sensitivity. If you have implanted devices, pacemakers, or are in advanced pregnancy, consult a clinician first. We cannot provide medical advice; contact your provider for individual clearance. Are sessions suitable for first-timers?
Yes. Facilitators will give brief orientation. You may rest, sit, or leave at any time. There is no required practice level.
Do you use the same instruments each time?
We curate instrument palettes intentionally. Examples we rotate: tuning forks and monochord for precise frequency work, rainstick for grounding textures, and tempered bell tones for articulation. Each event's mix is chosen for its therapeutic intent.

Session flow (brief)

If you hear tones differently

Some people notice ringing or phasing for hours after — this is usually benign but if it persists, seek medical guidance. Bring earplugs if you know you are sensitive.
