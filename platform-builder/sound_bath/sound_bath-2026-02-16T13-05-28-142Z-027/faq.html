Questions you may have — answered simply

A small compendium of practical notes for first-timers and regulars alike.

What should I bring and wear?
Wear comfortable layers. Bring a blanket, pillow/bolster, and an eye mask if you prefer darkness. Socks or warm feet are recommended; shoes are removed in the studio.
Is a sound bath a religious ceremony?
No. Our sessions are non-denominational. They are designed as therapeutic and restorative gatherings centered around breath, body, and listening.
Are sound baths safe? Any contraindications?
Most people find sound baths restful, but they are not suitable for everyone. Contraindications include epilepsy, recent concussion, pregnancy (especially in late stages), implanted medical devices such as pacemakers, or acute psychiatric conditions. If you have concerns, please reach out before attending at {{EMAIL}}.
Will I fall asleep?
Many participants drift into deep relaxation or sleep — that is entirely fine. The session structure accounts for rest; you'll be gently guided back at the close.
What is the flow of a session?
Typically: arrival and seating (10–15 min), a short grounding practice (breath or body scan), the sound journey (30–45 min) where instruments like gongs, chimes, or crystal bowls are layered, followed by a gradual return and light closing (10–15 min). Each session may vary slightly.
Is there social time afterwards?
Yes — we usually offer tea and a chance to linger for a few minutes. It's optional and a gentle way to reintegrate.

Quick logistics

Arrival: please come 10–12 minutes early to settle. Sessions begin promptly to preserve the experience for everyone.
