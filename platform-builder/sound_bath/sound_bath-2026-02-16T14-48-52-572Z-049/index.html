The ritual — arriving together

We begin by grounding: soft breath, brief check-in, and the tuning of the space. The practice is slow, sensory, and intentionally gentle — a held container for release and restoration.

  • Arrival: settle with a warm blanket and optional herbal tea.
  • Orientation: short whispered guidance to settle the nervous system.
  • Sound immersion: layered instruments lead you through shifting textures of tone.
  • Embers: slow reintroduction of ambient noise and silent reflection.

What to expect — a sensory map

Sessions last 75—90 minutes. We hold space seated or lying down with light blankets and cushions. Listen, surrender, return.

Instruments

Layered bowls, bowed monochord, light chimes, and subtle tuning forks weave the field. Each series may feature a gong or crystal bowls for a deeper arc.

Flow

  1. Welcome & gentle breathwork
  2. Guided visualisation & intention
  3. Deep tonal immersion (30–50 minutes)
  4. Soft landing & silent integration

What to bring

  • A yoga mat or something to lie on
  • Blanket, warm socks, and an eye pillow if you like
  • Water bottle
Contraindications: If you have epilepsy, recent head injury, or are pregnant, please consult with a medical professional before attending. Sound can shift the nervous system; inquire if you have implants or are on sensitive medication.

Questions people bring

How should I prepare?

Wear comfortable layers and arrive 10–15 minutes early to settle. Avoid heavy meals immediately before the session.

Can I fall asleep?

Yes. Rest is part of the practice. You’ll be gently guided back at the close so you can re-orient before leaving.

Is this therapy?

Sound baths are a supportive, sensory practice but not a substitute for medical or mental health treatment.
