Myth vs. Truth — A gentle unravelling

Myth: Sound baths are only about relaxation.

Truth: They can be restorative, somatic, and catalytic — a ceremony for nervous system regulation and subtle transformation.

Myth: You must meditate perfectly to benefit.

Truth: Ease of presence is enough. The sound holds you whether you rest, drift, or fall asleep.

Myth: All sessions use the same instruments.

Truth: We choose textures intentionally — crystal bowls shimmer, a gentle gong rounds the architecture, and hand-played chimes map light through silence.

Case notes — How a session unfolds

A typical group sound bath follows a quiet, repeating architecture that allows bodies to settle and resonance to weave.

  1. Arrival & grounding: soft tea, short orientation, gentle breathwork.
  2. Opening tone & intention: a single hum to set the communal pulse.
  3. Layered sound journey: instruments breathe in patterns — harmonics, shimmer, and a slow deep gong wash.
  4. Closure & integration: a bell, soft touchstones, and time to journal or share quietly.

Typical instruments here: crystal singing bowls, a small gong, hand chimes and soft rattle. (Each event's texture is curated.)
