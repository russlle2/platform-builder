Frequently Asked Questions

How can a Sound Bath specifically help me as an athlete managing physical stress?
As an athlete, your body endures a lot, and often, the emotional stress of performance, injury, or recovery can be overlooked. Our Sound Bath sessions provide a dedicated space for your nervous system to downregulate, helping you to release not just physical tension, but also the emotional residue from intense training or competition. It's a way to process underlying stress and promote a deeper, more restorative form of recovery beyond just physical rest.
I'm not used to 'sitting still.' Will a Sound Bath still be beneficial for my active mind?
Absolutely. It's common for active individuals to find stillness challenging, and that's perfectly normal. During a Sound Bath, you simply lie down and listen. The sounds themselves provide a focal point, gently guiding your mind away from its usual busy patterns without demanding active participation. Many athletes find this passive engagement incredibly effective for quieting an overactive mind and achieving a state of mental calm that's often elusive during their busy routines.
What should I expect during a Sound Bath session, and do I need to do anything special?
You'll lie comfortably on a mat, often with blankets and pillows for support. The practitioner will play various instruments, creating an immersive soundscape. Your only 'task' is to relax and allow the sounds to wash over you. There's no right or wrong way to experience it; simply be open to the sensations. Many people report feeling deeply relaxed, experiencing emotional releases, or gaining mental clarity. Just come as you are, ready to receive.
How does a trauma-informed approach apply to Sound Baths for athletes?
Our trauma-informed approach recognizes that intense physical experiences, injuries, or even the pressure of competition can sometimes create emotional imprints. We create a safe, non-judgmental environment where you feel in control. This means offering clear communication, respecting your boundaries, and understanding that emotional processing can happen gently and at your own pace. The focus is on supporting your nervous system's ability to self-regulate, fostering a sense of safety and inner calm as you navigate and release stress.
