Frequently asked questions

Clear, practical answers to common questions. If you don't see your question, email us at {{EMAIL}}.

Do I need experience with sound baths?

Not at all. Our sessions are intentionally accessible. We give verbal guidance on settling and posture; participants are invited to rest, sleep, or meditate as desired.

How long is a typical session?

Community sessions usually last 75–90 minutes (including arrival & integration). Private sessions may be 60–120 minutes depending on format.

What should I bring?

Layered clothing, a water bottle, and if you like, a small eye pillow. We provide blankets, bolsters, and mats. If you have a preferred cushion, bring it for extra comfort.

Are there any contraindications?

While sound baths are low-risk, please consult your physician if you have: a pacemaker or implanted electrical device, active seizure disorder, recent concussion, or are in the first trimester of pregnancy. If you have psychiatric conditions, or are on heavy medication, reach out before booking for a conversation. This is informative, not medical advice.

Can I bring someone with mobility needs?

Yes. We make space for chairs, mats, and companion support. If mobility assistance is needed, please contact us before the event so we can prepare the venue.

What instruments do you use?

We select instruments to suit each session's intention. Common choices include gong, monochord resonance, and a variety of hand-played percussion. Instruments are tuned with intention, and dynamics are moderated for comfort.

Booking & cancellations

Full refund up to 72 hours before an event. Within 72 hours we offer credit toward another session. Private bookings follow a separate cancellation policy communicated at booking.

Short guide: session flow

Arrival and grounding, main sound arc of 40–60 minutes, then integration. Expect a mix of long sustained tones and subtle percussive layers to guide the nervous system from sympathetic to parasympathetic states.

If I fall asleep?

Sleeping is common and a sign the nervous system is down-regulating — it's fine. We'll offer soft cues at the closing.

Accessibility and individual accommodations

We can adapt room layout, provide extra bolsters, allow a companion, or offer seated options. For hearing sensitivity, we reduce low-frequency volume and provide a quieter seat near the exit. Email {{EMAIL}} or call {{PHONE}} to discuss needs in advance.
