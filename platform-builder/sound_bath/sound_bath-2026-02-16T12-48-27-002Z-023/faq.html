Questions We Hear Often

What should I bring to a sound bath?
A yoga mat or cushioned pad, a blanket for warmth, a small pillow for neck support, and optionally an eye mask. We provide limited bolsters and blankets but recommend bringing your own for hygienic comfort.
Is a sound bath the same as meditation?
They overlap. A sound bath uses intentional sound layers to support meditative states; facilitation includes guided settling and reorientation, not just passive listening.
Who should avoid attending?
Contraindications: If you are pregnant (first consult your provider), have a history of epilepsy, photosensitive conditions, or are currently experiencing uncontrolled psychiatric crises, please consult with your clinician before attending. If you have implanted medical devices sensitive to vibration, contact us beforehand at {{EMAIL}}.
How loud will it be?
Volumes are intentional and monitored. We use instruments like gongs, tuning forks and soft crystal bowls — dynamic but never unsafe. You can request a lower placement in the room for reduced exposure.
What happens if I feel emotional or overwhelmed?
Facilitators are trained to support grounding. We pause the sound, offer verbal guidance and create space for regulated breath. If you need further assistance, we provide local resource referrals upon request.
Can I record or take photos?
To preserve the collective container, recording and photography are not permitted unless pre-arranged for accessibility reasons.

Session Flow Snapshot

  1. Check-in & brief intake (5 min)
  2. Guided settling (10 min)
  3. Soundset with layered instruments — gong, tuning forks, chimes (30–40 min)
  4. Slow reorientation & optional sharing (10 min)

Preparing

Wear comfortable clothing, avoid heavy meals within an hour of the session, and refrain from intense exercise immediately beforehand.
