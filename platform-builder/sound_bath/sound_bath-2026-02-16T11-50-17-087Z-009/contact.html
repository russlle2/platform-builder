Myth vs truth — what a sound bath actually does

Common myth

Sound baths are "mystical cures" that instantly fix deep trauma, or that participants must fall asleep to benefit.

Clinical truth

Sound baths guide the nervous system toward parasympathetic states — they support deep rest and regulation. Effects build with consistent practice; integration follows reflection and rest after the session.

Our pillars

Embodied listening — we use breath and posture cues to improve receptivity and comfort.
Intentional instrumentation — each tone is selected to layer harmonics and gentle overtones without overstimulation.

Frequently asked — practical details

What should I bring?
Bring a yoga mat, two blankets (one for warmth), a small pillow, and optionally a warm water bottle. Wear comfortable layers; noise-free clothing is helpful. If you do not have equipment, let us know and we will provide limited mats and blankets.
Contraindications — who should check with a physician?
If you are pregnant, have epilepsy, recent surgery, have a pacemaker, or are currently experiencing significant cardiovascular issues, please consult your healthcare provider before attending and note it in your message. We can design alternatives or recommend a private session with modifications.
What is the flow of a typical group session?
Doors open and soft lighting for 10–15 minutes for settling. Facilitator offers grounding breathwork and intention-setting, then the sound arc (45–60 minutes) with layered instrumentation. Session closes with low tones and a brief spoken return, followed by a quiet window for journaling or soft conversation.
