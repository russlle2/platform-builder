Frequently Asked Questions

How does Private Practice Therapy specifically address physical pain caused by college anxiety?
When you're under academic pressure, your body often reacts with muscle tension, headaches, or digestive upset. My Private Practice Therapy approach uses somatic techniques to help you identify where you hold this stress in your body. We then work to gently release that physical tension, teaching your nervous system to self-regulate more effectively, which directly alleviates pain and discomfort.
I feel constantly overwhelmed by academic deadlines; can Private Practice Therapy help with the physical exhaustion that comes with it?
Absolutely. The mental load of college deadlines often translates into physical exhaustion and chronic fatigue. In Private Practice Therapy, we'll explore how your body is holding onto this overwhelm. By employing somatic practices, you'll learn to release stored tension and regulate your nervous system, which can significantly reduce feelings of physical exhaustion and help you regain energy.
Will Private Practice Therapy teach me strategies I can use during exams or presentations to reduce physical anxiety?
Yes, that's a core component of our work in Private Practice Therapy. We'll practice specific somatic techniques and grounding exercises that you can immediately apply when facing high-stress situations like exams or presentations. These tools are designed to quickly calm your nervous system and reduce physical symptoms of anxiety, helping you feel more present and focused.
Is Private Practice Therapy a long-term commitment, or can I get practical tools for immediate relief?
While deeper shifts can take time, Private Practice Therapy is structured to provide you with practical, actionable tools for immediate relief from physical anxiety and stress. Our initial sessions will focus on understanding your current stressors and equipping you with techniques you can use right away. We'll then build upon these for more sustained well-being, tailored to your pace and needs as a college student.
