A clinical and contemplative approach to therapy

I blend relational therapy, evidence-informed strategies, and simple contemplative practices to help clients increase capacity and clarity.

Session types: Individual • Cohorts • Brief Packages

Diagnostic intake — how we begin

The intake is a careful diagnostic conversation. It attends to safety, functioning, and what a reasonable scope of work would be. I use a collaborative formulation to orient our plan together.

  • Safety & risk assessment
  • Current stressors and supports
  • Short- and long-term goals

Designing a plan

Plans are bespoke but often fall into three shapes: brief focused work, open-ended individual therapy, or cohort-based learning. Below is an illustrative pathway I use for clients who prefer structured learning.

Transition Cohort (illustrative)

  • Intake & goal-setting
  • Weekly thematic group sessions (8–10 people)
  • Two short individual check-ins
  • Home micro-practices and integration work

Cohorts emphasize skill-building and shared learning while maintaining clinical attention to individual needs.

Micro-habits and rituals

Small, consistent actions shift nervous system tone over time. Examples I offer clients:

  • 2-minute morning anchor: three slow diaphragmatic breaths
  • Boundary rehearsal: practice a short phrase to protect time
  • Midday body check: notice tension, breathe, stretch
