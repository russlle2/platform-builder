Where we begin: a short diagnostic conversation

I start with a 20–30 minute intake call to understand your current concerns, history, and immediate priorities. This is a collaborative listening space — we’ll clarify what feels most pressing and whether therapy with me is a good fit.

What I ask

Current symptoms, relationships, coping strategies, previous care, and practical logistics (schedule, accessibility, insurance/fees).

Timeframe

Many people notice measurable shifts in 8–12 sessions when we pair skills and reflective work. Your path may be shorter or longer — we make an individualized plan together.

Care boundaries

If there’s an immediate safety crisis I will help connect you with emergency services. I do not provide crisis response; call local emergency services or crisis lines in urgent situations.

A clear plan, co-created

After intake I propose a working plan: objectives, meeting cadence, and practical exercises tailored to your life. Here’s how we typically structure care.

  1. Phase 1 — Stabilize: Safety, symptom reduction, sleep and self-care basics.
  2. Phase 2 — Skill-building: Emotion regulation, communication techniques, trauma-informed processing.
  3. Phase 3 — Integration: Habit changes, relationship practice, relapse prevention.

We review progress every 4–6 sessions and adjust goals. You remain the primary decision-maker.

Practical daily moves

Small, consistent changes are often more effective than intense but short-lived efforts. These micro-habits are commonly recommended in early therapy.

  • Two-minute check-in

    Breathe for two minutes and notice three sensations: temperature, posture, and breath rhythm.

  • Mini exposure

    Approach a mildly uncomfortable situation for 5–10 minutes to reduce avoidance over time.

  • Connection pause

    Send one brief caring message to a trusted person each week to remind yourself you’re supported.

  • Evening inventory

    Note one thing that went better than yesterday — a practice in noticing progress.
