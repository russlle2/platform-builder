Common questions — practical answers

Clear, direct information to help you decide whether to reach out. If you don’t see your question, email {{EMAIL}} or book a short consult.

How do I start? What happens during the first visit?

First we’ll have either a brief consult (10–20 minutes) or a standard intake. The intake focuses on history, immediate concerns, and collaborative goals. We’ll discuss confidentiality, timeframes, and what you can expect from sessions.

Do you offer remote or in-person sessions?

I offer telehealth across {{STATE}} and limited in-person availability in {{CITY}}. Telehealth is convenient for many clients and remains private and secure. We’ll review technology needs in your intake.

Can I use insurance?

I provide superbills that you can submit to your insurance for out-of-network reimbursement in many cases. I do not directly bill insurance from this practice. Insurance may require diagnosis and carries its own records; we can discuss what that means for your privacy.

What if I’m between sessions or need urgent support?

I do not provide crisis services. If you are in immediate danger or feeling suicidal, call 911, your local emergency number, or a crisis hotline. For non-urgent concerns between sessions, message me and I’ll respond when possible; I may suggest resources or a timely check-in.

Do you accept minors or couples?

My practice primarily serves adults. I sometimes work with couples and families depending on needs and licensing scope. If you’re looking for specialized family work, I’ll discuss appropriate referrals.

How long does therapy usually last?

Every path is different. Some clients prefer short-term, goal-focused work (6–12 sessions); others benefit from longer-term relational therapy. We’ll review progress regularly and adapt the plan.

Ethics & privacy

Your privacy is central. I will describe limits of confidentiality, record-keeping, and mandatory reporting during intake. Communications outside of sessions (email/text) are not fully secure; I’ll review safer options for sensitive information.
