Questions I often hear

Below are practical answers to help you decide if this practice is a good fit. If your question isn’t listed, please reach out by phone or email.

How do I know if therapy will help me?
Therapy is a collaboration. While no outcome can be guaranteed, many people notice reduced distress, improved coping, and clearer values when they attend regularly and practice between sessions. We’ll set measurable goals together and reassess periodically.
What happens in the first session?
The first appointment focuses on listening: background, immediate concerns, safety, and goals. We’ll discuss logistics (scheduling, fees, confidentiality) and determine an initial plan. It’s also an opportunity to see whether our working style feels right.
Can I use insurance?
I am typically out-of-network. I provide a detailed receipt (superbill) for you to submit to your insurer. Coverage depends on your plan. We can discuss documentation needs during intake.
Are sessions confidential?
Yes, with important limits. Confidentiality is maintained except in cases required by law (e.g., imminent harm to self or others, child or elder abuse, court orders). Full privacy practices are provided at intake.
How long will I need therapy?
It varies. Some people benefit from short-term, solution-focused work; others find longer-term therapy supports deeper change. We’ll review progress together and adjust the plan as needed.

Facts vs. common myths

Myth: Therapy is only for crises.
Fact: Therapy is useful for coping, growth, and prevention — not just emergencies.
Myth: The therapist will tell me what to do.
Fact: I guide with questions, evidence-based tools, and collaborative planning so you decide what fits your life.
