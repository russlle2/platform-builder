My working method — steady experiments, practical gains

I treat therapy like iterative problem-solving: form a hypothesis, test a small change, review data, and refine. This keeps work focused and time-efficient.

1
Clarify an aim

We choose 1–2 concrete goals and identify how you’ll know they’re improving.

2
Design small experiments

Short, manageable exercises or shifts you can try between sessions.

3
Review data & adapt

We discuss what changed, troubleshoot barriers, and refine the next step.

4
Consolidate skills

When progress is steady, we focus on generalizing gains into daily routines.

Case: reducing evening rumination

Plan: 3 small nightly experiments (breathing practice, pre-sleep checklist, environmental cue). Outcome: reported fewer intrusive thoughts and better sleep consolidation after 5 weeks.

Case: reconnecting after conflict

Plan: practice short repair scripts and reflective listening in set moments. Outcome: improved capacity to de-escalate and clearer expectations.

What this approach is — and is not

Not a therapy of endless talk

We balance understanding with concrete practice.

Not a promise

Change is collaborative and varies by context; I cannot guarantee outcomes.

Logistics & scope

How will progress be tracked?

We choose 1–2 brief measures (e.g., frequency of panic thoughts, sleep efficiency) and review them periodically.

What if my needs change?

We revise goals together and I will make referrals if a different level or specialty of care is needed.

Pathways

"Focused Pathway": 6 sessions with measurable goals. "Open-Ended" for deeper exploration. Sliding thoughtfully considered on a case-by-case basis.
