Questions people often ask

Below are practical answers from a clinician’s perspective. If you don’t see your question, email {{EMAIL}} and I’ll reply within a few business days.

How do cohorts differ from individual therapy?

Think of cohorts as learning cohorts: shared curriculum, group discussion, and periodic individual check-ins. They are excellent for building skills and steady practices. Individual therapy offers private clinical attention tailored to your personal history and safety needs.

Can I use insurance?

I do not bill insurance directly. I can provide a superbill (receipt) you may submit to your carrier. Coverage depends on your plan. If insurance is required, I can suggest clinicians who accept it.

What if I’m in crisis?

I do not provide crisis services. If you are in immediate danger, call emergency services. For urgent mental health crises, contact local crisis lines or 988 if you are in the U.S. If you are unsure, call your local emergency number.

How is confidentiality handled?

Sessions are protected under clinical confidentiality. Exceptions include imminent risk of harm to self or others, suspected abuse of a vulnerable person, or other legally required reporting. We’ll review limits of confidentiality during our intake.

What if I can’t afford standard rates?

I try to keep a limited number of sliding-scale openings and offer cohort scholarships when possible. Cost should not be a barrier—reach out and we can discuss options candidly.

Do you work with medication management?

I do not prescribe medications. If medication evaluation is something you need, I can coordinate with or refer to prescribers as part of a collaborative plan.

How long before I notice change?

Every person’s timeline is different. Some people feel clearer after a few sessions; others benefit from longer engagement. My approach prioritizes practical steps we can test together. There are no guarantees, only a shared process.

Still unsure?

You can choose a single intake session to explore fit and plan a path forward — that can include cohort participation or a sequence of private sessions.
