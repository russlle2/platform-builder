How I work: a hypothesis-driven, compassionate model

I treat therapy like testing an intervention: we form a hypothesis, try practical steps, measure change, and iterate. This keeps us grounded in what actually helps you in daily life.

Core phases of engagement

Assessment
— clear problem formulation, values, and target symptoms.
Intervention
— skills, behavioral experiments, pacing.
Measurement
— tracking outcomes, adjusting course.
Integration
— relapse prevention, community resources.

Evidence and flexibility

I prioritize approaches with empirical support (CBT, exposure, DBT skills, trauma-sensitive interventions) while adapting to your preferences, cultural context, and readiness. We test gently and respect pacing.

Myth vs. nuance

Myth: Clinicians always know the right answer.
Nuance: I bring frameworks and hypotheses; your feedback and real-world outcomes determine what we keep doing.
Myth: Therapy should make you feel better immediately.
Nuance: Sometimes progress is non-linear. Short-term discomfort can be part of long-term gains; we plan for stabilization and build safety nets.

Assessment tools I use

  • Validated symptom measures (brief questionnaires)
  • Behavioral tracking and exposure hierarchies
  • Collaborative progress reviews every 4–6 sessions

Session formats & candidacy

I offer standard 50-minute psychotherapy sessions, extended sessions by arrangement, and short consultations. Therapy is appropriate for non-emergency mental health concerns. For crisis support call emergency services.
