How we work together: structure, practices, and boundaries

Daily micro-practices — tiny acts, continuous effect

I emphasize short, repeatable exercises that fit into busy lives. These precede deeper processing so regulation becomes safer and more reliable.

Grounding pause (60–120s)
Orient to five senses; breathe; name one safe object. Use before difficult calls or to end rumination.
Check-in journal (3 min)
A daily 3-line log: energy, one win, one need. Builds awareness and guides session focus.
Boundary experiment
A small, trackable request (eg. 1 sentence) tested in low-stakes contexts to grow relational confidence.

Diagnostic stance

I avoid rushing to diagnosis. Our initial focus is safety, functioning, and how symptoms affect relationships and daily work. Assessment is iterative and informed by your lived experience.

  • Session 1–3: history, safety, immediate supports.
  • Session 4–8: targeted interventions and measurable goals.
  • Longer arcs: processing and meaning-making as capacities grow.

Care plan examples

Plans are created together. Common pathways include:

  • Stabilization track: skill-first, 6–10 sessions.
  • Therapeutic track: weekly sessions, deeper relational and trauma work.
  • Maintenance check-ins: biweekly or monthly as skills consolidate.

Therapeutic metaphors I often use

I invite clients to think of therapy as both map and practice: the map (assessment) helps us see patterns; practice (micro-habits + experiments) changes the nervous system over time. This balance keeps work grounded and actionable.
