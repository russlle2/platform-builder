Questions people commonly bring before starting therapy

Below are practical answers to help you decide if we might be a good fit. If you don’t see your question, please reach out.

How do I know if therapy is the right step now?
If distress is interfering with relationships, work, sleep, or meaning in life, therapy can offer a space to explore change. We begin by clarifying small, achievable aims and establish a simple plan to see if the work feels helpful.
What happens in an initial appointment?
The intake is a 60-minute assessment: we review history, current concerns, risk and safety, and collaboratively set a treatment focus. You will receive an outline of recommended next steps and scheduling options.
Do you accept insurance?
I operate primarily on a private-pay basis and can provide a detailed superbill for insurance reimbursement when appropriate. I do not submit claims directly on a client’s behalf. Sliding-scale spaces are limited.
How do we communicate between sessions?
I keep contact outside sessions minimal. Administrative emails and scheduling are fine; for clinical matters we reserve discussion for sessions except in specified safety concerns. Boundaries are important to therapeutic work and are discussed in the first sessions.
What if I’m in crisis?
If you are in imminent danger or unable to keep yourself safe, call local emergency services or your crisis line right away. For non-urgent concerns, please use the office contact. I do not provide emergency services.
How long will therapy take?
It varies. Some people engage in short-term work (8–12 sessions) with focused goals. Others choose longer-term, open-ended therapy. We regularly review progress and adjust the pace together.

A brief self-diagnostic

Consider these three questions and bring your reflections to the intake: 1) What feels most urgent? 2) What is one small change you want in 6 weeks? 3) What might make therapy feel successful for you?
