Anxiety & worry

Practical cognitive-behavioral strategies paired with exposure and acceptance skills when helpful. Focus on disrupting avoidance and increasing valued activity.

Typical work: structure for panic prevention, graded exposure, and skills for daily worry management.

Trauma aftereffects

Trauma-informed pacing, safety planning, and stabilization come first. When appropriate, targeted processing and integration tools are used with consent.

Typical work: distress tolerance, grounding, and paced processing to reduce reactivity.

Relationship patterns

We map interaction cycles and practice communication skills. Therapy focuses on clarity of needs, boundaries, and repairing ruptures when present.

Life transitions & career shifts

Decisions often involve values, emotion, and logistics. I help clients clarify priorities and take stepwise actions aligned with goals.

Programs and ways to work together

Focused Path (brief): 6–12 sessions for targeted problems with clear measurable goals. Often selected for a discrete issue like panic or a separation plan.

Stability Plan: Monthly sessions plus skills check-ins for clients wanting ongoing maintenance and relapse prevention.

Flexible Sessions: Single sessions for review, safety planning, or therapy checkpoints.

Short examples of outcomes

Example: A client reduced nightly ruminative worry by structuring a "wind-down" plan and testing sleep hygiene, reporting improved rest in six weeks.

Example: Couples work focused on practical communication led to clearer agreements about responsibilities and decreased conflict escalation.

Questions people often ask

Will I be judged?

Therapy here is nonjudgmental; sessions prioritize curiosity and skillful feedback to help you reach your goals.

How fast will I see changes?

Change varies. Many clients notice behavioral shifts in weeks; deeper relational patterns take months. We track metrics together.
