Questions people often bring up before booking

Below are practical answers to help you decide if working together is a fit. If you prefer, schedule a short consult and we can cover these in conversation.

1) How do you approach therapy with trauma?
I use trauma-informed techniques that prioritize safety and pacing. We co-create a plan that includes stabilization skills first, and only move into deeper processing when you feel reasonably safe and supported.
2) Will you diagnose me?
Assessment is part of early sessions when clinically relevant. Labels can be useful for shared language, but my focus is on patterns, functioning, and practical steps rather than on a single diagnostic category.
3) Can I call between sessions?
Brief administrative messages by email or secure messaging are fine. For urgent issues, we will agree a plan during intake; in crisis you should contact emergency services or local crisis resources.
4) How long until I notice change?
Change varies. Some people feel relief in a few sessions when learning new tools; deeper shifts often require sustained attention over months. We check progress regularly and adapt the plan.
5) What are your boundaries around confidentiality?
Sessions are confidential within legal and ethical limits. Exceptions include imminent risk to self or others, suspected abuse of a minor or vulnerable adult, or court order. We review these limits together in intake.
6) Do you work with couples or families?
My primary focus is individual adult therapy. For couples or family work I will either adapt sessions and expectations or refer to a specialist when appropriate.
