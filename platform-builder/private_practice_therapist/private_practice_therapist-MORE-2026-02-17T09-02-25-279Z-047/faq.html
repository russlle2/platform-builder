Practical questions answered

Short, clinician-forward answers about what to expect, confidentiality, and how appointments are handled. If something here doesn’t fit your situation, email {{EMAIL}}.

How do I book and pay?+
You can book online at the scheduling link or email to request a time. Payment is taken at the time of service via card. For packages, payment is collected up front for the block so we can hold regular sessions without interruption.
What is your cancellation policy?+
Please provide at least 48 hours notice when possible. Late cancellations or missed appointments may be billed the session fee. If difficulty with this policy would create a barrier for you, we can discuss options before booking.
Telehealth and location+
Most sessions are offered via secure telehealth. Limited in-person meetings may be available; ask about current availability. We’ll confirm platform details when you schedule.
Confidentiality and scope−
I treat what we discuss with professional confidentiality. Limits to confidentiality include situations where there is a reasonable concern about imminent harm to yourself or others, known abuse of a child or vulnerable adult, or if I am ordered by a court to release records. I will always try to be transparent about these limits and discuss them with you when they are relevant. Records: I keep basic clinical records about sessions. If you request copies, I can provide a summary and discuss any concerns about information sharing. Email and texting: Email and SMS are not fully secure. Use them for scheduling or brief messages only. For sensitive content, we use the secure telehealth platform or discuss during sessions.
Insurance and superbills+
I do not bill insurance directly. I can provide a superbill with the necessary codes so you may submit for out-of-network reimbursement if your plan allows. Coverage and reimbursement are determined by your carrier.
What won’t therapy do?+
Therapy is collaborative and often gradual. I cannot guarantee specific outcomes. We work together to clarify goals, test approaches, and notice what helps. If your needs require another type of service (e.g., specialized medical care or an urgent safety plan), I will discuss appropriate referrals.
If you are in immediate danger or require urgent medical attention, contact local emergency services right away. If you are having suicidal thoughts or feeling you might harm yourself, please call your local emergency number or the national crisis line. If you are in {{STATE}} and need additional help, reach out to local crisis resources, or call your primary medical emergency number. If you have questions about safety planning, bring them up in the first session or email {{EMAIL}}.
Clinical note: This information describes typical practice procedures and important limits to confidentiality. It is not a substitute for individualized assessment or emergency support. If you have immediate safety concerns, please call for help.
