A pragmatic, collaborative process

Therapy as a sequence of shared experiments: we identify hypotheses, test practical strategies, and adjust based on what helps.

1. Clarify
We map concerns, values, and current patterns so goals are specific and testable.
2. Stabilize
Build safety, reduce arousal, and create predictable routines—especially after trauma or crisis.
3. Practice
Introduce skills (CBT, exposure, interpersonal experiments) and practice them between sessions.
4. Reflect & integrate
We review outcomes, consolidate gains, and plan for maintenance or stepped care.

A science-informed stance

I lean on empirically supported methods while respecting individual variation and context. That means using techniques supported by research but adapting pace and emphasis to fit you.

I avoid overpromising. Therapy can change patterns and increase well-being, but results depend on many factors including social supports, life circumstances, and sustained practice.

Brief case example — collaborative targets

Context: Client struggled with recurring social anxiety impacting work meetings.
Plan: 8-week Steady Start package: behavioral experiments, graded exposure to meetings, and cognitive restructuring.
Result: increased meeting attendance and reduced avoidance; client reported more balanced appraisal of performance.

Myth vs. practical truth

Myth: Therapy is a one-size-fits-all method.
Practical truth: We choose and adapt techniques based on goals, readiness, and response. Flexibility increases the chance of helpful change.

FAQ — approach-specific

Will you tell me what to do?
I collaborate. Sometimes I offer specific skill practices or behavioral experiments; other times we use inquiry to help you discover a next step.
How will we measure progress?
We use goal tracking, session reviews, and client-reported measures when useful. Progress is considered through changes in functioning and distress.
What if therapy isn't helping?
We will re-evaluate formulation, consider alternative methods, or recommend additional resources or referrals when appropriate.
