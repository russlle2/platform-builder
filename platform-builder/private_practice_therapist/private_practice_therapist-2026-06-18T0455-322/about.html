What kind of physical pain can a Private Practice Therapist help with during postpartum?

As a Private Practice Therapist, I specialize in addressing common postpartum physical discomforts such as pelvic pain, back and neck tension from feeding or carrying your baby, wrist pain, and generalized body aches. We focus on gentle somatic techniques to release tension and restore comfort in your body.

How is somatic healing different from other therapies for new mothers?

Somatic healing, offered by your Private Practice Therapist, specifically focuses on the body's sensations and physical experiences. Instead of just talking about feelings, we explore how emotions and past experiences manifest in your body, allowing for a deeper release of tension and a more integrated sense of well-being, particularly relevant to the physical changes of postpartum.

Will I need to talk extensively about my birth experience with the Private Practice Therapist?

While your birth experience is often an important context for your current physical and emotional state, you are never pressured to share more than you feel comfortable with. As your Private Practice Therapist, I honor your pace and your story. We can explore the physical sensations and emotional echoes of your birth through gentle somatic inquiry, without needing to recount every detail if you prefer not to.

How soon after giving birth can I start working with a Private Practice Therapist for somatic healing?

It's generally recommended to wait until you have received clearance from your medical provider, typically around 6 weeks postpartum, before beginning some forms of bodywork or somatic healing. However, gentle inquiry and emotional processing can often begin sooner. Your Private Practice Therapist will discuss your individual circumstances and ensure our approach is safe and supportive for your unique recovery.
