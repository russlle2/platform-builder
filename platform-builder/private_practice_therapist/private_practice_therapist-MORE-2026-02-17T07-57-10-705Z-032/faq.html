Frequently asked questions

Concise answers to practical questions about membership, billing, confidentiality, and access.

How do memberships begin?
We start with a brief intake session (30–50 minutes) to understand needs, clarify goals, and confirm a membership plan. That intake is followed by an agreement that outlines frequency, billing, and messaging expectations.
What if I need a shorter, time-limited plan?
Time-limited, goal-focused programs are available and framed as a single plan (e.g., 6–12 sessions). These are useful when there is a specific topic or transition to address. We’ll discuss suitability during the intake.
Do you take insurance?
I offer private-pay membership billing and provide receipts you can submit to your insurance for possible reimbursement. Coverage varies; check with your carrier. I can discuss documentation on request.
How is confidentiality handled?
Records are maintained according to professional standards. Confidentiality is respected except in legally defined situations (e.g., imminent risk of harm to self/others, suspected abuse, or court orders). We’ll review limits to confidentiality before beginning clinical work.
What if I’m in crisis?
This practice does not provide emergency/crisis services. If you are in immediate danger or experiencing an emergency, call local emergency services or your local crisis line. If you are in the U.S., you can also dial 988 for the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline.
How do cancellations and scheduling work?
Memberships include a scheduling agreement that outlines notice periods for cancellations and how to request additional sessions. If you need to reschedule, please contact the office within the agreed window so we can find a suitable time.
What is the therapeutic scope?
I work with adults on a range of personal, relational, and life-transition concerns. I do not provide forensic evaluations, court testimony, or emergency medical care. If your needs fall outside my scope, I’ll provide referrals to appropriate resources.
