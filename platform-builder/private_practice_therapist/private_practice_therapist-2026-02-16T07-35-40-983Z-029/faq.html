Common questions, answered simply

Practical details to help you know what to expect. If you don’t see your question, reach out — I’ll answer directly.

How quickly can I get in for an appointment?
I maintain a small, managed caseload to allow timely scheduling. New clients are typically offered intake appointments within 2–4 weeks; for urgent needs, I will try to find an earlier slot or brief phone consultation.
Do you take insurance?
I do not bill insurance directly. I provide itemized superbills that you can submit to your insurer for possible out-of-network reimbursement. Payment is usually taken at the time of service (card or HSA/FSA).
What can I expect in the first few sessions?
The first session focuses on understanding your immediate concerns, history, and goals. We collaboratively decide if short-term focused work or ongoing therapy fits best. You can expect a calm, practical approach with clear next steps.
How do you handle emergencies or crisis situations?
I am not an emergency service. If you are actively in danger, call 911 or go to your nearest emergency room. For urgent mental health support in the U.S., call or text 988. In-session confidentiality has legal limits if someone is at imminent risk of harm.
Can you work with couples/families?
I offer relationship-focused work for partners when there is shared commitment to therapy. I also see individuals who are navigating relationship concerns. I will discuss expectations, roles, and confidentiality at intake.

A few short policies

Confidentiality is maintained within legal and ethical limits. Sessions are private unless you sign a release. I do not provide forensic evaluations or court testimony. My scope includes anxiety, trauma-informed care, bereavement, burnout, and life transitions.
