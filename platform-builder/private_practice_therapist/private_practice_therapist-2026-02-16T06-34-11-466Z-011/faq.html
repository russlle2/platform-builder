Practical Questions

1) How do I know if we’re a good fit?
We begin with a 50–60 minute intake to explore goals, prior care, safety, and expectations. Fit is mutual; if it seems we’re not the best match I will discuss referrals. Clinical fit includes modality ({{MODALITIES}}) and shared values about pace and focus.
2) Can I use insurance?
I provide superbills for out-of-network reimbursement in many plans. I do not typically bill insurance directly. If you have questions about documentation, we can address them during intake.
3) What happens if I need to cancel?
I ask for 48 hours notice for cancellations. Late cancellations or missed sessions may incur a fee, except in genuine emergencies. We will review the cancellation policy before beginning ongoing work.
4) Are our conversations private?
Yes—confidentiality is foundational. Exceptions include imminent risk of harm to self/others, suspected abuse of a minor or dependent adult, or a court order. I will discuss limits to confidentiality during intake and answer any questions you have.
5) How do sessions typically flow?
Sessions are collaborative: check-in, review of what emerged since we last met, focused work for the agreed-upon goal, and a brief plan for between-session practices. I integrate practical tools and relational reflection rather than homework that feels burdensome.
6) Do you work with couples or families?
I see couples for relationship-focused work and occasionally work with family members when relevant. We discuss goals, roles, and confidentiality up front. Complex family systems sometimes require referral to a specialist.

Care Notes & Boundaries

If you are in crisis or having thoughts of harming yourself or others, please call local emergency services or the nearest crisis line. Therapy supports are not immediate crisis response. I maintain secure clinical records and follow professional ethical standards about privacy and reporting obligations.

Short Practices Between Sessions

Small, sustainable practices that people often find helpful:

  • Two-minute grounding breath when reactive
  • One-line reflection in the evening about what shifted
  • Brief self-compassion phrases in moments of self-judgment
