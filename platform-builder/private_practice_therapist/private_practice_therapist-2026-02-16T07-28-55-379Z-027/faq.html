Practical answers before your first session

Below are common questions clients ask when considering therapy with me. If you don't see your question, reach out at {{EMAIL}}.

Core commitments

Clear goals, mutual respect, and transparent boundaries are central. I am guided by ethics and clinical standards.

How do I know if I should start therapy now?
If an issue is interfering with daily functioning, relationships, or your sense of well-being, it may be a good time to seek support. We can start with a single consultation to clarify focus and fit.
What modalities do you use?
I draw from CBT, emotion-focused work, trauma-informed practices including EMDR-informed strategies, and relational approaches. I tailor methods to {{MODALITIES}} and your needs.
Do you offer telehealth?
Yes. I provide secure teletherapy across state lines when permissible and in-person sessions in {{CITY}}, {{STATE}}. We'll confirm modality at booking.
Can I use insurance?
I do not bill insurance directly. Clients may receive a superbill to submit for out-of-network reimbursement. Sliding scale is available in select cases.
What should I expect at the first visit?
We review your history, set goals, and co-create an initial plan. I ask about safety, strengths, and practical obstacles to help structure work.
Is what I say confidential?
Yes — communications are confidential within professional and legal limits. Exceptions include clear risk of harm to self or others, or subpoenas. More detail is available at intake.

Tools you'll likely use

Short behavioral experiments, grounding practices, and structured reflections between sessions help accelerate change.

Concerns I often address

Time commitment

You set session frequency; many begin weekly and then move to biweekly as progress stabilizes.

Stigma or embarrassment

Therapy is a resource for learning — embarrassment often fades quickly when you experience practical tools and support.
