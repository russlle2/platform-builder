Myths I often hear — and what’s actually helpful

Myth

Therapy is only for crises.

Truth

Therapy can be preventive and growth-oriented. Much of the work is skill-building and perspective shifts that reduce future distress.

Myth

You need to know what’s "wrong" to start.

Truth

Uncertainty and feeling stuck are valid reasons to seek support. We begin with what matters to you now.

Myth

Therapy gives immediate solutions.

Truth

Therapy is a gradual process. I focus on measurable steps, not promises, to help you gain stability and clarity.

Foundational principles I use in sessions

  • Collaborative assessment: We clarify what’s happening together and set concrete goals.
  • Skill-focused work: Practical tools (emotion regulation, behavioral experiments) you can use between sessions.
  • Context-aware care: Attention to relationships, environment, and identity that shape symptoms or distress.

Common questions

How do I know if therapy is a good fit?
We start with a brief consultation to review your concerns, what’s worked before, and what you hope to change. That conversation helps both of us determine fit and plan initial steps.
What modalities do you use?
I integrate cognitive-behavioral strategies, trauma-informed approaches, and interpersonal frameworks. See full list on the specialties page under "{{MODALITIES}}".
Do you accept insurance?
I offer a mix of private-pay and limited insurance billing. Please ask at intake so we can review options and any necessary documentation.
What is the typical session cadence?
Most clients begin weekly, then shift to biweekly as progress stabilizes. We tailor frequency to your needs.

Privacy, scope, and safety

Confidentiality: Sessions and communications are confidential, with exceptions required by law (e.g., imminent risk of harm to self or others). Electronic communications are not fully secure; please note this when sharing sensitive details.

Scope and boundaries: I provide psychotherapy for adults. I do not provide emergency services; if you are in crisis or at immediate risk, call local emergency services or your crisis line. For urgent help in the U.S., call 988 or your local emergency number.

Crisis disclaimer: Contacting me does not establish an emergency response. If you are experiencing severe symptoms that impair safety or daily functioning, please seek immediate local care.
