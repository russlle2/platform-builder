Areas of focus

I primarily work with adults using integrative, research-aware practices. Below are common presenting concerns and how I typically orient treatment.

Anxiety and worry

Short-term skills (breathing, behavioral experiments) combined with sustained cognitive restructuring when needed. We prioritize interventions you can test in real life.

Trauma-informed care

Stabilization and safety are first. I draw from phase-based frameworks and somatic awareness for clients affected by earlier life stressors.

Relationships & communication

Work on patterns of attachment, conflict, and boundary-setting; practical skill rehearsal in session and between sessions.

Life transitions & career shifts

Decision-making frameworks, values clarification, and paced behavioral changes to reduce overwhelm during big transitions.

Burnout & workplace stress

Assessing workload, meaning, and daily habits; building micro-boundaries and restorative routines.

Depressive symptoms

Behavioral activation, problem-solving, and collaborative monitoring to reduce withdrawal and increase valued activity.

Common clarifications

I aim to demystify the process. These short clarifications help set expectations.

  • Timeframe: Some goals are short-term (8–12 sessions); others are longer, depending on complexity.
  • Outcome: I cannot promise specific results; we monitor progress together and adjust methods.
  • Privacy: Records are confidential. Required disclosures are rare and follow legal guidelines.

How I describe therapy to new clients

Therapy is an iterative experiment: we form hypotheses about what sustains distress, try manageable changes, and collect evidence on what helps. That pragmatic stance guides my work.
