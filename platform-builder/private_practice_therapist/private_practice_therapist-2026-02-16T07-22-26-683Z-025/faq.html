Practical answers for beginning therapy

A concise orientation so you can decide whether to reach out. If something here is unclear, email {{EMAIL}}.

How do I know if therapy is right for me?
I help adults seeking support for anxiety, trauma responses, relationships, transitions, and professional burnout. Therapy is a collaboration; our initial sessions are an opportunity to assess fit and clarify goals. There is no obligation beyond the intake conversation.
How long do people usually work together?
Duration varies. Some people meet briefly for a focused problem (6–12 sessions); others work longer to address deeper patterns. We will discuss a tentative plan during intake and revisit it regularly.
Do you accept insurance?
I am an out-of-network provider. I provide superbills that you can submit to your insurer for potential reimbursement. Coverage varies; please check with your plan. See the Fees page for rates and package options.
What happens if I’m in crisis?
I do not provide crisis services. If you are in immediate danger or at risk of harm, call local emergency services or your local crisis line. If you are in the U.S., you can call 988 for the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline.
How is confidentiality handled?
Sessions are confidential within legal and ethical boundaries (for example, risk of harm, abuse, or court-ordered disclosures). I will provide a detailed confidentiality statement during intake and answer any questions you have before starting.
Quick intake snapshot
Before our first session, you will complete a short intake form describing presenting concerns, recent stressors, and basic health history. This helps me prepare and makes our time together more focused.
Micro-practices to start now
  • Pause: 3 slow breaths when you notice intense emotion.
  • Anchor: name two things you see and two sounds you hear for grounding.
  • Reflect: jot down one small value-driven action to try this week.
