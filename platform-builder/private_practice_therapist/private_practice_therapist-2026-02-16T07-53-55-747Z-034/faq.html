Questions people often ask before starting

Below are answers I give most often. If you don't see what you need, reach out — I'm happy to clarify things by email or phone.

New here?
Download a brief worksheet: "How to prepare for your first three sessions." It outlines questions I ask and ways to get the most from our time.
What kinds of concerns do you work with?
I routinely work with anxiety, depression, trauma-informed care, relationship challenges, career and life transitions, perfectionism, and burnout. I work with adults and consider referrals for specialty needs when appropriate.
Do you take insurance?
I do not bill insurance directly. Many clients submit superbills to their insurance for out-of-network reimbursement; I can provide these upon request. We can discuss finances confidentially.
How do I know if therapy is working?
We set collaborative goals and review progress periodically. Progress looks different for everyone — sometimes it's symptom reduction, sometimes increased clarity or better relationship boundaries. If something isn't helping, we adjust the plan.
Can I change session frequency or format?
Yes. Sessions can move between weekly, biweekly, or occasionally monthly. I offer telehealth broadly and limited in-person appointments in {{CITY}}. We set a cadence that suits your goals and schedule.
What if I need immediate help or am suicidal?
If you are in immediate danger or have active suicidal plans, call emergency services or your local crisis line. If you are in the U.S., call 988 for the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline. I do not provide crisis care via email; if it is urgent, contact local emergency resources.
How do cancellations and rescheduling work?
I require 24 hours notice for cancellations. Late cancellations or missed sessions may be billed at the full session rate unless there are exceptional circumstances. We will review policies at intake.
Are sessions recorded or notes kept?
I keep clinical notes for treatment continuity; they are confidential and stored securely. I do not record sessions without explicit consent and a clear clinical reason.
How long does an initial intake take?
An initial intake typically lasts 50–75 minutes. We'll review history, priorities, and agree on a plan. Sometimes I offer a brief 15-minute consult to answer administrative questions before booking.
