What will happen in the first appointment?

The first session is an intake: we review your main concerns, relevant history, strengths, and immediate goals. I also cover confidentiality, logistics, and together we explore whether my approach feels like a fit. Expect to leave with a sense of next steps rather than a finished plan.

How long does therapy usually take?

There is no fixed timeline. Some people come for a short series of sessions focused on a transition; others engage over months for deeper work. We’ll revisit goals periodically to ensure the work remains useful and efficient.

Do you offer telehealth or in-person options?

I offer secure telehealth as a standard option and limited in-person slots when available. For remote care, I provide guidance on creating a private space and preparing for a virtual session.

Is what I say kept private?

Yes. Sessions and clinical records are protected. Exceptions include risk of harm to self/others, suspected abuse, or legal compulsion. I will discuss these limits openly during intake and answer any questions about privacy protections.

Can I use insurance?

I do not bill insurance directly. I can provide a superbill you may submit to your insurer for possible out-of-network reimbursement. If insurance is essential, I can help you consider other local clinicians who accept it.

What if I’m in crisis?

I do not provide crisis services. If you are in immediate danger, please call 911 or your local emergency number. For non‑life‑threatening crises, consider contacting your local crisis line or the 988 Lifeline (US). If you’re a current client and need urgent help, reach out and I will advise appropriate resources.
