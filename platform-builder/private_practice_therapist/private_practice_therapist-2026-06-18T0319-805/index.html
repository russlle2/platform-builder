Finding Your Center Amidst Change

Life transitions can feel disorienting, like you've lost your anchor. Our approach as your Private Practice Therapist helps you re-establish an internal sense of stability by tuning into your physical self. Through guided awareness and gentle movement, you'll learn to identify and alleviate the physical manifestations of stress, allowing you to move through changes with greater ease and a renewed sense of self-possession. It's about finding your personal equilibrium.

It's easy to overlook your physical stance when your mind is racing with new challenges. Yet, consciously engaging with your posture can be a powerful tool for emotional regulation. Frequently Asked Questions

How can body awareness help me cope with a big life change?
Big life changes often show up physically as tension, fatigue, or changes in how you carry yourself. By becoming more aware of your body, you can identify these physical signals, understand what they're telling you, and learn techniques to release that tension, helping you feel more grounded and less overwhelmed during transitions.
Is this approach suitable if I'm not very flexible or active?
Absolutely. Our focus isn't on strenuous exercise or achieving specific poses. It's about gentle, mindful attention to your body's current state and capacity. We tailor all guidance to your comfort level and physical abilities, ensuring a supportive and accessible experience, regardless of your activity level.
What does a session focusing on postural health look like?
A session might involve guided awareness exercises where you notice how you're sitting or standing, gentle movement to release areas of tension, or discussions about how stress impacts your physical posture. It's a collaborative space to explore your body's communication and find strategies for greater comfort and stability.
Will I need any special equipment for this type of Private Practice Therapy?
No, you won't need any special equipment. Our work together primarily uses your own body and your immediate environment. Sometimes a comfortable chair or a quiet space is helpful, but the core tools are your attention and your willingness to connect with your physical self.
