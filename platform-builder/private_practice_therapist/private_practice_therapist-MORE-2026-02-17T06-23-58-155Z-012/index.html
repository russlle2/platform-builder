Myth vs. What helps

Myth: Therapy fixes you.

Reality: Therapy is a collaborative space to explore patterns, try alternatives, and build safer habits. It takes time and curiosity—not a quick repair.

Myth: You must know your problem to start.

Reality: You can begin with a sense of unease or a question. Clarity often arrives through steady, focused work.

Myth: Therapy always means long-term weekly sessions.

Reality: I offer short, focused pathways and small cohort options alongside individual work so you can match time and intensity to your needs.

How I structure support

Focused Pathways

6–8 week short-series with a clear practical aim: decision points, adjustment after a loss, or stress management. Ideal for people wanting momentum with check-ins.

Small Cohorts

Time-limited groups (cohorts) where you test strategies alongside peers with shared goals. Designed to normalize ideas while keeping clinical care tight.

Individual Sessions

One-to-one work for deeper exploration, safety planning, and skill practice. We co-create boundaries and rhythms that fit your life and commitments.

What a short pathway looks like

  1. Intake conversation: clarify goals and safety.
  2. Set a 6-week focus: a tangible aim to practice in between sessions.
  3. Weekly check-ins: 45–60 minutes to review experiments and adjust.
  4. Conclude with a review and options for next steps (short follow-up session offered).

Pathways are flexible—things change, and we notice together.

Boundaries, privacy, and safety

I maintain confidentiality within legal and ethical limits. I document sessions for clinical purposes. If you have specific privacy concerns (work, shared accounts, billing), tell me early so we can plan alternatives.

I work within a psychotherapy scope. I do not provide legal, financial, or medical diagnoses beyond a clinical assessment. When needs fall outside my training, I offer referrals and coordinate care as needed.

Sessions are set appointments with agreed start/end times. I ask for 24–48 hours notice for cancellations. We discuss availability and typical response times for messages—urgent concerns are handled differently (see crisis guidance below).

If you are in immediate danger: call your local emergency number right away. If you are experiencing an urgent mental health crisis and are not in immediate danger, consider contacting local crisis services or a certified crisis line in your area.

I cannot provide crisis services outside scheduled appointments. If you need immediate support between sessions, please use emergency services or a crisis line.
