Questions people often bring up

Concise, practical answers to help orient you before we talk.
How do I know if therapy is a good fit?
Therapy often feels useful when you notice patterns that cause recurring distress, or when a life change feels harder to manage alone. The first few sessions are about assessing fit, goals, and practical steps. There is no obligation to continue beyond an initial meeting.
How do confidentiality and records work?
Your sessions are confidential. Notes are kept securely and in alignment with professional ethics. Exceptions exist where safety is at risk (harm to self or others) or as required by law; these limits are discussed during intake.
What therapeutic modalities do you use?
I draw from {{MODALITIES}} and integrate evidence-informed approaches tailored to your needs. Our work is collaborative and adapted over time.
Can I switch to telehealth or in-person?
Yes. Telehealth is available across {{STATE}} except where restricted by licensure. In-person sessions are offered in {{CITY}} when scheduled. We will confirm logistics on booking.
What should I prepare for my first session?
Think about what brings you now, your immediate goals, and any recent stressors. I will ask brief administrative and clinical questions to form a shared plan. No prior preparation is required.

Clarifying a few persistent myths

Therapy = weakness?
Actually, seeking help is an active, courageous step toward skill-building and well-being.
One-size-fits-all?
Treatment is tailored. We co-create goals and methods that fit your life and values.
Immediate cures?
Therapy is a process. Progress is measurable but rarely instantaneous.
