Questions people often ask

Short answers to help you decide if this practice might be a good fit. If something here is unclear, please reach out by phone or email.

Who do you work with?

I work with adults navigating life transitions, relationships, and patterns of stress. Sessions are collaborative and tailored — we focus on what you want to change and what resources you already have.

What can I expect in a first visit?

The first meeting is an opportunity to describe your concerns, ask questions about approach, and try out the therapeutic fit. We'll define early goals and agree on practical next steps.

How long are sessions and how often?

Standard sessions last about 50 minutes. Frequency varies — weekly, every-other-week, or short series work, depending on goals and schedule.

Do you take insurance?

This practice operates on a private-pay basis. If you have out-of-network benefits, I can provide a receipt you can submit to your insurer. I do not bill insurance directly.

What if I need help right away?

This practice does not provide crisis services. If you are in immediate danger, please call emergency services or your local crisis line. If you're unsure where to turn, consider contacting your primary care or local crisis resources.

Can we meet remotely?

Yes. Telehealth visits are available and often work well for continuity. We will discuss privacy practices and technical needs during scheduling.

Boundaries & scope

I provide psychotherapy and brief consults. I do not offer forensic evaluations, emergency crisis services, or medical management. If a referral is needed, I will help connect you with appropriate resources.

When therapy doesn’t fit: some practical boundaries

Therapy is a space for reflection and skill-building. There are times when other services are more appropriate — medical care, emergency psychiatry, or peer-run crisis services. I will be transparent if a different resource would better meet your current needs.

Confidentiality note: Information you share is confidential except where disclosure is required by law (risk of harm, abuse, or court order). This site is informational and not a substitute for clinical care. If you are in acute crisis, call emergency services or your local crisis number.
