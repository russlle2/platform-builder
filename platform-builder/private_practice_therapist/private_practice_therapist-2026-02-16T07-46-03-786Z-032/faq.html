Practical answers for getting started

Below are the questions I commonly hear from people considering therapy. If something isn’t listed, reach out — I’m happy to clarify.

Safety first: we set clear boundaries and plans for distress.
Collaborative approach: your goals shape the work.
Transparent logistics: clear info about scheduling and fees.
How do I start — what happens at the first appointment?
The first session is an assessment: we review your history, current concerns, and what you hope to change. We also discuss confidentiality, crisis planning, and whether my approach fits your needs.
Which therapeutic approaches do you use?
I integrate evidence-informed methods including elements of {{MODALITIES}} with a relational lens. I tailor interventions to what helps you build stability and move toward goals.
Do you accept insurance?
I am a private-pay clinician. I provide superbills that you may submit to your insurer for out-of-network reimbursement. Insurance coverage varies — please check with your carrier.
What if I need to cancel?
I ask for 48 hours’ notice for cancellations when possible. Late cancellations or missed sessions may be billed; we review this during intake so expectations are clear.
Is therapy confidential?
Yes. Your information is kept private within legal and ethical limits (e.g., imminent risk, court orders). I will describe confidentiality and recordkeeping at our first meeting.
What if I’m in crisis between sessions?
I do not provide crisis services by message. If you are in immediate danger, please call emergency services or your local crisis line. We will create a safety plan during intake.
How long will therapy take?
It varies. Some people seek short-term work for a specific issue; others opt for ongoing therapy. We review progress regularly and adjust the plan together.
Do you offer packages or sliding scale?
I offer limited sliding-scale spots and occasional short packages designed for focused outcomes (e.g., a 6-session "Foundations" track). See the Fees page for current options or ask during the consult.
