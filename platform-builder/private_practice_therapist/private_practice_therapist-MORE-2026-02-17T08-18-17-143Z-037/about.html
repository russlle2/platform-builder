A short story about why I do this work

A grounded invitation

People come with a tangle of feelings and practical problems—work that matters, relationships that need attention, and fatigue that lowers the bandwidth for change. My role is to hold a steady, informed presence that helps you sort priorities, test small steps, and notice what's actually working.

Clinical perspective

How I structure sessions

I integrate evidence-informed approaches in a flexible way tailored to your concerns. We map understandable next steps together and make room for the real-life constraints you bring. Progress looks different for everyone — we'll track what matters to you, not a preset checklist.

Boundaries & confidentiality

Important things to know

Your conversations are held in confidence following professional ethics and legal requirements. I also discuss limits to confidentiality (for example, if there is risk of harm) and will work transparently about them. If you are in crisis or at imminent risk, please contact emergency services or your local crisis line immediately—therapy is not a substitute for emergency care.

Who I work with

Typical focuses

Adults navigating transitions, persistent anxiety or low mood, relationship shifts, life-stage decisions, and the ripple effects of stress. If you are unsure whether therapy is right for you, a brief consult or message can help clarify fit.

Practice logistics

Session format

I offer both in-person appointments in {{CITY}} and secure teletherapy. We discuss session frequency together; some clients prefer weekly support while others choose biweekly sessions. Payment, insurance, and cancellation policies are reviewed during intake.

Courtship before commitment

A simple next step

If you want to explore working together, the easiest way is to request an initial consult. That meeting is an opportunity to describe what brought you here, ask practical questions, and see if the fit feels right—no pressure, just clear information.
