Practical answers about process, scope, and safety

Below are common questions clients ask before they begin. If you don't see an answer, reach out — I'm glad to clarify.

Journey & methods

Which therapeutic approaches do you use?
I draw from evidence-informed methods—trauma-informed cognitive-behavioral strategies, somatic awareness, and interpersonal frameworks—selected collaboratively to fit your goals.
How long does therapy usually take?
There's no fixed timeline. Some people find relief in short-term focused work (6–12 sessions); others benefit from longer-term exploration. We set measurable goals and periodically review progress.
Are sessions confidential?
Yes: clinical notes and conversations are confidential within professional limits. Exceptions include imminent risk of harm, court orders, or mandated reporting. A full privacy statement is included in the intake materials.
What should I expect at intake?
The first appointment combines clinical history, goal-setting, and practical orientation (scheduling, fees, emergency plans). It is an opportunity both to assess fit and to begin work.
What if I am in crisis?
I do not provide crisis services. If you are in immediate danger, please call local emergency services or a crisis hotline. If you are suicidal or feel unsafe, contact 988 (U.S.) or your local emergency number.
Do you work with couples or families?
My primary practice focuses on individual adult psychotherapy. For couples or family therapy I may offer referrals or limited co-therapy options depending on training and fit.

Measuring progress: what I track

We use symptom tracking, goal review, and periodic check-ins about life functioning. If something isn't moving, we adapt the plan. Therapy is iterative and data-informed rather than one-size-fits-all.

Common misconceptions clarified

Therapy requires full vulnerability right away. I pace disclosure and skills work to what feels safe and useful.

I will tell you what to do. My role is to guide and teach skills; decisions remain yours.

Core pillars of my work

  • Safety first: practical interventions when distress is high
  • Curiosity: hypothesis-driven exploration
  • Skill-building: concrete practices you can use
