Questions I Hear Often — Practical Answers

Below are common questions about how I work, what to expect in sessions, and logistics. If your question isn’t here, reach out via {{EMAIL}}.

How do I know if therapy is the right next step for me?

Many people begin therapy when a pattern shows up in relationships, work, or mood. My approach is exploratory and collaborative — we’ll clarify goals together and check in regularly about progress.

Do you accept insurance or provide receipts?

I am primarily private-pay. I can provide a superbill on request to submit to your insurance for possible out-of-network reimbursement. Submission does not guarantee reimbursement.

What if I’m between therapists or just need a few sessions?

I offer short-term and open-ended work. Some clients come for targeted skill-building (4–8 sessions); others continue monthly check-ins. We’ll design a plan that fits your needs.

How do you handle confidentiality and records?

Records and communications are kept confidential within legal and ethical boundaries. If there are limits (e.g., imminent risk of harm), I will explain them at intake. Ask for a copy of the privacy policy if helpful.

What should I expect in my first session?

We’ll cover your history, current concerns, what you hope to change, and practical logistics like frequency and fees. Often clients leave with an initial plan and a small practice to try between sessions.

Cancellation and rescheduling policy?

I request 48 hours’ notice for cancellations when possible. Late cancellations or no-shows may be billed at a reduced rate. If you need flexibility, please tell me — I’ll do my best to work with you.

I’m in crisis — can I reach you?

I do not provide emergency services. If you are in immediate danger or require urgent help, please call emergency services or a crisis line in your area. If you are in the U.S., you can call 988 for the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline.

My therapeutic framing — the ANCHOR method

ANCHOR is a structure I use: Attend, Notice, Contextualize, Hold, Orient, Reflect. It helps keep therapy steadied so you can try new ways of responding while feeling contained.

Programs & sample offerings

Short Path (4 sessions): focused skills and coping; Couples Care (ongoing): relationship check-ins; Transition Support (6–12 sessions): life changes such as career shifts, parenting transitions, or relocation.
Confidentiality & privacy: Your information is protected in line with clinical practice standards. Crisis: I do not provide crisis services. Scope: I provide psychotherapy only; I do not prescribe medication.
